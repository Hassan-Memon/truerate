How TrueRate decides

Why rates differ everywhere

There is no single "true" exchange rate. Banks trade with each other at the interbank rate. A money changer quotes a buying and a selling rate on either side of it. Central banks publish an official reference once a day. Websites copy one of these, at different times, and round differently.

So instead of pretending to know the exact number, TrueRate shows the consensus and the spread. If sources agree within 0.2%, the number is solid. If they disagree by 3%, you deserve to know that before you move money.

The rules

  1. Consensus = median of the live market feeds. A median ignores one bad feed.
  2. Range = lowest to highest feed, shown as a percentage spread.
  3. Official = Frankfurter's consensus of central-bank quotes, plus the issuing bank's own figure when it exists, even if it is days old (marked stale).
  4. Trust score starts at 100 and loses points for few sources, wide spread, a gap between market and official, and stale data.
  5. Cross rates (say PKR→SAR) are built per source, so each source's own internal consistency is kept.

Street (kerb) rates from money changers are not in any free public feed yet. When one appears, it will be added as its own tier.

Data: ExchangeRate-API, Currency-API, FloatRates, MoneyConvert, and ~100 central banks via Frankfurter. Not financial advice. Rates are informational; the rate you get from a bank or money changer will differ.
