How TrueRate works

Why rates differ

There is no single true exchange rate. Banks trade with each other at the interbank rate. Banks and money changers quote their customers a buying and a selling rate around it. Websites copy one of these, at different times.

So we measure your deal against a benchmark, show how good that benchmark is, and say so when it is not good enough.

The rules

  1. Cost is what you would have received at the benchmark, minus what you did receive.
  2. For today we use the median of the live market feeds. For past dates we use central-bank figures for that date.
  3. Under 1% is close to market, 1–3% is noticeable, 3% or more is high, and 25% or more is very high. These bands are our judgement; 3% is the UN target for the cost of sending money home.
  4. Differences inside the margin of error get no verdict.
  5. If sources split into separate groups, or a currency is documented as having more than one rate, we give no rate and no verdict.
  6. Cash deals get no verdict yet, because dealers publish their own prices. We show the difference from mid-market and the published dealer price we have.
  7. Figures more than three days old are marked. Unknowns say "not yet documented".

Common questions

Why is the rate I got different from the one on Google?

Websites usually show a mid-market rate, a reference figure between buying and selling prices. Banks, apps and money changers quote you their own rate around it, so the rate you get will normally be different.

What is the interbank rate in Pakistan?

It is the price at which banks trade dollars with each other through brokers. The State Bank of Pakistan publishes a daily closing average. Customers do not trade at it directly: banks and exchange companies quote their own rates.

How do you work out what my deal cost?

We multiply what you gave, including any fee, by the benchmark rate for that date, then subtract what you received. We also state the margin of error, and give no verdict when the difference is inside it.

Do you see or store my amounts?

No. Your browser asks us only for the rate for a currency pair and a date, then does the calculation itself. Your amounts are not sent to us and nothing is stored.

Why is there no verdict on cash deals?

Cash dealers publish their own buying and selling prices on either side of the mid-market rate, so comparing them with that rate can make a poor deal look fair. Until we have fresh dealer prices, we show the difference and no verdict.
